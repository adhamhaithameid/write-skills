# Reader load

The mission: reading the reply must never feel like homework. This file holds two weapons: the seven patterns that slow readers down, and the anti-homework rules.

The seven patterns come from a study of 14 language models by NYU and UT Austin researchers, reported by The Decoder in October 2026. The study ties the patterns to training that rewards correctness, not clarity. Fix them on sight.

## The seven patterns

### B23. Rare pairs

Two words that rarely appear together. The reader stops to work out the relationship.

Before: "a reader-patience budget"
After: "a limit on how long readers wait"

### B24. Long gaps

The subject and its verb sit far apart. Working memory strains across the gap.

Before:
```text
A cache that always keeps the hottest entries in memory and never evicts
them until the budget is exhausted is the default.
```
After:
```text
The default cache keeps the hottest entries in memory. It evicts them
only when the budget runs out.
```

### B25. Dropped connectors

A relative clause loses its "that" or "which". The reader must re-parse.

Before: "the book the professor wrote"
After: "the book that the professor wrote"

### B26. Tacked nouns

Three or more nouns in a row. The reader guesses which word modifies which.

Before: "horror movie soundtrack generator"
After: "a generator for horror-movie soundtracks"

### B27. Nouns as verbs

A noun gets used as a verb.

Before: "calendar it" / "let's architecture this"
After: "put it in the calendar" / "let's design this"

### B28. Things as people

An inanimate thing gets a verb of a living being.

Before: "The conversation is eating its own tail." / "The database wants more memory."
After: "The argument repeats itself." / "The database needs more memory."

### B29. Flipped roles

The tool acts and the human receives. The reader came to act, not to be acted on.

Before:
```text
The function allows you to filter the results. This API offers you the
ability to delete a key.
```
After:
```text
You can filter the results with the function. You delete a key with one
call.
```

## The anti-homework rules

A reply can pass every rule and still read like an assignment. The signs, and the fix:

1. **Announced structure.** "In this reply I will cover three points." The reader wants the points, not the plan. Delete the announcement; start with point one.
2. **Section ceremony.** Numbered sections and headings for a reply that fits one screen. Structure earns its place past 3 distinct sections, never before.
3. **The closing summary.** A paragraph that repeats what the last paragraphs said. The one exception: a long turn may end with the short TLDR block.
4. **The bullet wall.** Ten bullets where three sentences carried the argument. Prose carries an argument. Bullets carry true lists.
5. **Uniform rhythm.** Every sentence the same length reads like a machine. Short sentence lands a point. Long sentence carries a fact with its condition.
6. **The quiz tone.** "You might be wondering...", "Let's dive in", "It's important to understand". Talk like a colleague at a desk, not a teacher at a board.

## The test

Read the draft once as a stranger. One question: did any part of it feel like work? Fix that part first.
