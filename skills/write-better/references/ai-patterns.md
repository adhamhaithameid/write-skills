# AI patterns to remove

Scan the draft for these patterns. Rewrite each match. Keep the meaning and the tone. The rule numbers are stable. A gap marks a removed rule.

## Content

- **B1** A superficial "-ing" phrase: "highlighting", "ensuring", "reflecting". Delete it, or replace it with the real fact.
- **B2** A vague attribution: "experts believe", "industry reports suggest". Name the source, or delete the claim.
- **B3** AI vocabulary: "additionally", "crucial", "delve", "enduring", "enhance", "fostering", "garner", "interplay", "intricate", "pivotal", "showcase", "testament", "underscore", "vibrant". Replace each with a plain word.

## Language

- **B4** A fancy way to say "is": "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Write "is" or "has".
- **B6** A forced group of three. Use the natural number of items.
- **B8** A false range: "from X to Y" with no real scale. List the topics.

## Style

- **B9** An em dash. Use a period or a comma. Do not use parentheses or dashes as a substitute.
- **B10** A colon as a mid-sentence connector. A colon is fine before a list or an example.
- **B11** Bold on every name or acronym. Stop.
- **B12** A bold label that restates the line. Convert it to prose.
- **B13** A title-case heading. Use sentence case.
- **B14** A decorative emoji in a heading or bullet. Remove it.
- **B15** Curly quotes. Use straight quotes.

## Talk artifacts

- **B16** A chatbot phrase: "I hope this helps", "let me know if", "Of course!", "Certainly!". Remove it and answer directly.

## Filler

- **B18** Filler and adverbs: "in order to" becomes "to". "due to the fact that" becomes "because". Delete "very", "really", "just", "actually", "basically". Use a stronger verb in place of an adverb.
- **B19** Excess hedging: "could potentially possibly be argued" becomes "may".
- **B20** A generic conclusion: "the future looks bright". State the specific plan or fact.

## Jargon

- **B21** An abstract metaphor noun: "substrate", "wedge", "vector", "nexus", "paradigm", "north star", "flywheel", "endgame". Write the concrete word: "base", "add", "way", "method", "goal", "the last phase".

## Plain speech

- **B22** Say what it does, not how it feels. Name the mechanism or the number. A sentence that could sit in any project says nothing. Cut it.

## Reader load

Seven patterns that slow readers down. The full examples and fixes live in `reader-load.md`.

- **B23** Rare pairs: two words that rarely appear together. Rewrite the relationship in plain words.
- **B24** Long gaps: the subject and its verb sit far apart. Move them together, or split the sentence.
- **B25** Dropped connectors: a relative clause lost its "that" or "which". Put the connector back.
- **B26** Tacked nouns: three or more nouns in a row. Add the small words that show the relations.
- **B27** Nouns as verbs: a noun used as a verb. Write the verb phrase.
- **B28** Things as people: an inanimate thing with a verb of a living being. State the need or the behavior plainly.
- **B29** Flipped roles: the tool acts and the human receives. Make the human the actor.
